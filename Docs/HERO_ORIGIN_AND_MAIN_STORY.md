# ALSAEQA — Canonical Hero Origin & Main Story Record

## Status
**Canonical story record — user-provided story canon, September 2026**

This document records the canonical story supplied for the hero and must be treated as the source of truth when implementing future cinematics, quests, characters, dialogue, progression, animation beats, and world events.

## 1. The Thunder Legacy

The hero comes from a ruling family known as the **Thunder Giants**.

**Thunder Giants does NOT mean literal giant-sized people.** It is the name of the ancient bloodline/family and is intended to evoke their extraordinary thunder-related power, authority, and legacy.

For centuries in the medieval era, the Thunder family ruled the realms associated with the Thunder Kingdom and protected their lands from an evil power seeking control of the world and its resources.

The family repeatedly stood against that evil force and prevented it from taking control.

## 2. The Fall of the Thunder Kingdom

The evil power eventually gathered enough strength to destroy the Thunder ruling family.

At the time of the attack, the hero — later known as **Alsaeqa / الصاعقة** — was only about **4–5 years old**.

During the assault:
- The enemy attacked the Thunder Kingdom.
- The hero's father was killed.
- The hero's brother was killed.
- The hero's mother hid the young child behind a chest/large storage box so that he would not immediately be found.
- The child secretly witnessed the destruction around him.
- The hero's mother and surviving siblings were captured by the attackers.
- The child escaped the immediate capture.

This childhood memory is traumatic and incomplete. The player should initially see fragments rather than receive the entire truth in exposition.

## 3. The Second Captors

After fleeing the attackers who destroyed his family, the child was eventually captured by **different criminals/slavers** who were not the same force responsible for destroying the Thunder Kingdom.

They enslaved him.

As he grew older, he was forced to work in mines and stone quarries alongside other enslaved workers.

## 4. The Young Hero Before Awakening

At the beginning of the playable-era story, the hero is approximately **22 years old**.

His body must reflect years of forced labor and hardship without becoming a stereotypical bodybuilder:
- Initially a believable average/fit young male physique.
- Not extremely muscular.
- Not extremely thin.
- Muscles develop naturally through progression and survival rather than appearing as exaggerated bodybuilder definition at the start.
- Body and face show fatigue, scars, dirt, wounds and the effects of years of abuse.
- Clothing is old, torn and visibly worn.
- His physical appearance gradually improves as the adventure progresses.
- Armor, clothing, equipment and overall silhouette evolve with progression.

The hero begins the story physically and emotionally broken down, but with an underlying resilience.

## 5. The Mine Collapse and the First Awakening

While working in the mines, a section collapses.

The hero falls into a deep underground hole.

Inside the hole he discovers an ancient object, mechanism, relic, symbol, or puzzle-like source connected to the Thunder lineage.

The hero already bears a **distinctive mark on his body**. The mark becomes important to the mystery of his identity and inheritance.

When he touches the ancient object, he feels a dormant power inside himself.

This is the first meaningful awakening of the abilities inherited from the Thunder family.

The first abilities should be survival/traversal-oriented rather than an instant transformation into an all-powerful warrior. For example, the awakening can manifest as an enhanced climbing/launching/grappling-like movement that allows him to escape the deep pit.

He then uses the newly awakened ability to escape the mine and flee his captors.

## 6. What the Player Knows at the Start

The hero **does not initially know that he is a descendant of the Thunder family**.

He also does not initially remember his true name.

The identity reveal must happen gradually.

The player first knows him through his present circumstances and the name **Alsaeqa / الصاعقة** as the intended canonical name, while the character himself does not understand the full meaning of his identity.

His childhood memories return in fragments over the course of the adventure.

## 7. Opening Cinematic Canon

The opening should not simply explain the backstory with narration.

It should use short, powerful memory fragments:

2. The ancient conflict with the evil power.
3. The attack on the Thunder Kingdom.
4. The young hero hiding behind the chest.
5. His father and brother being lost.
6. His mother and surviving siblings being captured.
7. The child's escape from the immediate attackers.
8. A short transition showing his later enslavement.
9. The young adult hero working in the mines.
10. The collapse.
11. The fall into the deep chamber.
12. Discovery of the ancient object.
13. The mark reacting.
14. First awakening of thunder power.
15. Escape from the mine.

The opening cinematic is intentionally a compressed emotional prologue. It must not reveal every mystery.

## 8. Opening Runtime and Structure

The existing opening-cinematic specification remains the production reference for camera language, audio, VFX and hand-off to Stage 1.

The new hero canon in this document overrides earlier ambiguity about:
- the hero's childhood age;
- the identity of the Thunder Giants;
- the distinction between the original attackers and later slavers;
- the hero's initial physical condition;
- the hero's gradual identity/memory reveal.

The condensed backstory/escape beat should be approximately **30 seconds when required by the final game flow**, while the complete cinematic presentation may use additional shots before handing control to Stage 1. The final cut must preserve the same canon and emotional sequence.

## 9. Stage 1 — Return to the Mines

The first major playable objective after the awakening is to **return to the mines**.

The hero returns not as a slave but as someone who has begun to discover his power.

His objective is to:
- confront the people responsible for enslaving the workers;
- rescue the workers still trapped in the mines;
- defeat the hostile forces controlling the mine;
- help the enslaved workers escape;
- begin uncovering the first clues about his own past.

This makes Stage 1 personally meaningful: the hero is not merely escaping for himself; he begins his journey by helping people who are suffering as he suffered.

## 10. Progressive Character Reveal

The hero's transformation must be gradual across the adventure.

### Early progression
- Worn clothing.
- Visible wounds and scars.
- Limited physical confidence.
- Basic weapons.
- First unstable thunder abilities.
- No full understanding of his ancestry.

### Middle progression
- Better equipment and armor.
- More controlled thunder abilities.
- Increasing physical strength and confidence.
- More memories of his mother, father and siblings.
- Recognition of symbols, places and family traditions.
- Increasing awareness that his past is connected to major events in the world.

### Later progression
- Mature combat style.
- Advanced weapons and armor.
- Mastery of inherited thunder abilities.
- Full understanding of the Thunder lineage.
- Connection between his family history and the larger conflict.
- Stronger emotional motivation to recover what remains of his family and confront the forces responsible.

The visual transformation must support the narrative rather than happen suddenly in one or two stages.

## 11. Memory System

Memories are a core storytelling mechanic.

The hero should experience fragmented memories involving:
- his father;
- his mother;
- his brothers/siblings;
- the Thunder Kingdom;
- family symbols;
- moments immediately before and during the fall of the kingdom;
- unexplained fragments that only become understandable later.

Memory scenes should be cinematic and emotionally motivated.

They should not reveal all answers at once.

Each recovered memory should either:
- answer an earlier mystery;
- create a new question;
- recontextualize a previous event;
- reveal a location/person/symbol;
- or deepen the hero's emotional motivation.

## 12. Weapons and Equipment Progression

The hero starts with simple equipment.

Weapons, armor, clothing, materials and visual quality must evolve with the adventure.

Progression should be visible in:
- weapon design;
- armor/clothing;
- materials;
- silhouette;
- scars and physical condition;
- combat animation;
- thunder effects;
- movement confidence.

The progression must never make the hero look fully equipped at the beginning.

## 13. Cinematic Character Performance

The hero's performance is a major part of the story.

Required emotional states include:
- childhood fear;
- shock;
- grief;
- confusion;
- exhaustion;
- pain;
- anger;
- determination;
- wonder when discovering the thunder power;
- fear during dangerous traversal;
- relief after rescue;
- trust and attachment toward companions;
- emotional reactions to recovered family memories;
- resolve during major confrontations.

Facial animation, body language, camera language, sound design, music and VFX should reinforce these states.

## 14. Canon Rules for Future Development

1. Thunder Giants are a **family/bloodline name**, not a literal race of giant-sized humans.
2. The hero was approximately **4–5 years old** during the fall of the Thunder Kingdom.
3. His father and brother were killed during that attack.
4. His mother and surviving siblings were captured.
5. The people who later enslaved the hero were a **different group** from the force that destroyed the Thunder Kingdom.
6. The hero spent years working under slavery in mines/quarries.
7. He is approximately **22 years old** when the main adventure begins.
8. His initial physique is believable and worn, not an exaggerated bodybuilder physique.
9. His clothing is initially ragged and damaged.
10. He carries a distinctive mark connected to his heritage.
11. The mine collapse leads to the first awakening.
12. The ancient object/relic triggers recognition of dormant inherited power.
13. He does not initially understand that he belongs to the Thunder lineage.
14. He does not initially remember his true name.
15. His memories return progressively.
16. His appearance, equipment and abilities evolve gradually.
17. Stage 1 begins with the mine/slavery storyline and leads into the wider adventure.
18. Future cinematics must preserve this canon unless the story is deliberately revised later by the project owner.

## 16. Companion Canon — Permanent Companion

The hero meets the permanent companion in **Stage 5**, immediately after freeing her from the enemies holding her captive. This is a fixed story event and takes precedence over earlier roadmap wording that places the first meaningful companion meeting later.

She is not a temporary follower. She has her own personal story, combat abilities, meaningful combat participation, dialogue, animations and cinematic development. Her family story remains a major arc, with the established family-search progression continuing through stages 25–40.

## 17. Temporary Companion Canon

Temporary companions are distinct characters who join the hero for specific high-intensity story stages where their skills, presence or circumstances justify needing an additional companion. They are not interchangeable copies of the permanent companion.

Their encounters should be placed according to story and stage needs, especially difficult combat, dangerous rescues, infiltration/escape, traversal challenges and major enemy strongholds. They may enter for a specific sequence, participate in combat and cinematics, and leave naturally when that story segment ends.

Where required by the story, their state must persist: rescued, alive, separated, lost, or available for later world interactions.

## 18. Companion Progression Rule

The campaign distinguishes:
1. **Hero** — central protagonist and Thunder heir.
2. **Permanent companion** — rescued in Stage 5, with her own story and combat role.
3. **Temporary companions** — encountered later at carefully selected high-intensity stages where additional skills or presence are narratively justified.

The number and timing of temporary companions must serve pacing and escalation rather than a repetitive schedule.
