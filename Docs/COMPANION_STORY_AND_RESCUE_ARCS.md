# Companion Story and Rescue Arcs

## Core rule
The permanent female companion is a second protagonist, not a follower NPC. Her story progresses alongside the hero's story and remains expandable after any major stage.

## Her personal journey
She begins as another enslaved survivor who is freed by the hero. Later she learns that her family disappeared while fleeing the same forces that hunted unusual bloodlines and ancient powers. She starts following clues about her family while helping the hero search for his own origins.

Her family mystery is deliberately separate from the hero's family mystery. Some clues overlap, but neither story exists only to serve the other.

## Capture and rescue system
The companion can be captured by enemies during selected story events. Capture does not permanently remove her from the game.

A capture arc works as follows:
1. The companion and hero enter an enemy-controlled region.
2. A scripted ambush, separation, or collapsing route splits them.
3. The hero escapes but discovers evidence that she was taken alive.
4. A later stage reveals her prison, convoy, fortress, mine, or ritual site.
5. The player investigates and chooses an infiltration or direct assault route.
6. The rescue becomes a playable set piece with guards, alarms, reinforcements, traps, and environmental hazards.
7. After the rescue, the companion returns with new information about her family and may reveal that her captors were searching for her abilities.

The rescue stage is not the end of her arc. It unlocks new threads and can change later encounters.

## Ability discovery
The companion does not start with all abilities. She discovers them through personal trials and story events.

Suggested progression:
- First discovery: heightened awareness of hidden paths and danger.
- Second discovery: a defensive or mobility skill awakened while protecting the hero or rescued prisoners.
- Third discovery: a combat ability revealed when she is separated from the hero.
- Fourth discovery: a family-linked ability revealed by an ancestral object or location.
- Later discoveries: advanced skills whose origins connect to her family's history.

Every new ability should have a scene, clue, trial, or consequence. Do not dump all abilities into the opening.

## Temporary separation
Some stages temporarily remove the companion from the active party because of capture, injury, a blocked route, or an independent objective. These are story transitions, not permanent deletion.

While separated, the hero can discover messages, footprints, dropped equipment, prisoner testimony, or markings left by her. The companion can also act independently off-screen and return with information that changes the next stage.

## Companion decisions
The companion has her own opinions and decisions. She may suggest routes, object to unnecessary cruelty, prioritize rescuing prisoners, insist on following a family clue, or choose to investigate a suspicious lead. These moments create dialogue and consequences without taking control away from the player during normal gameplay.

## Inter-stage non-interactive scenes
Between major playable sections, use short cinematic conversations and environmental events:
- campfire conversations;
- walking dialogue while crossing a safe route;
- companion noticing a family symbol;
- hero revealing part of his past;
- rescued workers discussing what they saw;
- a distant giant roar interrupting a conversation;
- enemy scouts observing the party from afar;
- companion quietly practicing a newly discovered skill;
- a family clue causing a sudden change in her behavior.

These scenes are non-interactive and should never be treated as stage completion. They are connective events inside the continuous world.

## Persistent and revisitable design
A stage remains available after its main objective. Later powers, weapons, rescues, and companion discoveries can update old areas.

Examples:
- A previously inaccessible family chamber opens after the companion discovers her ability.
- A rescued group establishes a safe camp that becomes a future dialogue and supply point.
- A prison visited earlier can later contain a new captive or a new clue.
- A giant that appeared in the distance can become a full encounter after the player gains the required traversal power.
- The companion can revisit an old location and recognize a family mark that was previously meaningless.

## Escalation
The companion's story should grow in parallel with the hero's:
- Early: survival and trust.
- Middle: family clues and first abilities.
- Later: capture and rescue, hidden lineage, advanced abilities.
- Endgame: her family mystery intersects with the ancient conflict without replacing the hero's central quest.

The system is intentionally open-ended so new companion arcs, captures, rescues, abilities, family members, and stages can be added without declaring the game finished at a fixed stage.
