# Companion Memory Flashbacks

## Narrative purpose
The permanent companion's childhood memories are revealed gradually. They explain her fear, motivations, family search, and later abilities without exposing the complete truth too early.

## Flashback design
Memories are short, non-interactive cinematic separators. They can trigger after environmental cues, family symbols, enemy insignia, imprisonment locations, thunder-like sounds, or major emotional events.

## Memory arc
### Memory 01 — The hiding place
As a small child, she hides in a cramped storage space while armed enemies enter her family's home. She hears her family being ordered outside but cannot reveal herself.

### Memory 02 — Taken alive
She sees a close family member being restrained and taken away. The memory ends before she can see where the captors go, creating the first long-term mystery.

### Memory 03 — The loss
From her hiding place she witnesses the death of another family member. The scene focuses on her reaction and the sudden silence afterward rather than graphic detail.

### Memory 04 — The final instruction
Before being separated from her, a family member gives her a short instruction to hide and survive. The words become meaningful much later when she finds the same symbol in another region.

### Memory 05 — The convoy
A later trigger restores a fragmented memory of prisoners being transported. One prisoner appears to recognize her, suggesting that at least one family member may have survived.

### Memory 06 — The wrong memory
She realizes that childhood fear caused parts of the memory to be incomplete. A location she believed was the place of a death may actually have been a transfer site.

### Memory 07 — The ability awakening
During a dangerous event, a latent ability activates. The ability is connected to a family trait, but its full origin is not revealed yet.

### Memory 08 — The family trail
After finding several clues, she remembers a route, landmark, or phrase that points toward a region not previously associated with her family.

## Gameplay connection
Each memory can unlock a clue, optional objective, dialogue variation, map marker, companion behavior, or future revisit event. Memories never simply exist as lore dumps.

## Emotional pacing
Memories should be separated by normal gameplay. Do not repeatedly interrupt action with flashbacks. Use silence, environmental audio, brief visual fragments, and character reactions to make important memories meaningful.

## Non-graphic presentation
The family violence is presented through implication, reaction, sound, silhouettes, interrupted views, and aftermath rather than graphic gore. The purpose is character development and mystery, not graphic depiction.

## Long-term payoff
The memory chain remains open after the first family rescue. New relatives, hidden records, survivors, enemy archives, and contradictory evidence can extend the companion's story into later stages.
