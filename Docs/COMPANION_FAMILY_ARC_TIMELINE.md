# Companion Family Arc — Very Late-Game Timeline

## Core rule
The permanent companion's **active search for her family begins at stage 25**. Before stage 25, the game must not turn her family mystery into a rescue/search quest.

Her family remains an emotional mystery in the background while the early and middle adventure focuses on survival, trust, her gradual ability awakening, the hero's lineage, weapons, exploration, and the escalating threat.

## Story pacing

### Early stages — 1–6
- The companion is introduced through the enslavement/rescue arc.
- She focuses on surviving and learning to trust the hero.
- Only very brief fragments of her past may appear.
- No active family-search mission.
- No family rescue objective is placed on the map.

### Middle stages — 7–12
- Small clues may appear: a symbol, memory fragment, old marking, or familiar enemy insignia.
- The companion may react to a clue, but the hero does **not** begin a family expedition.
- Her abilities continue to awaken through dedicated moments.
- Clues are background mysteries that can be revisited later.

### Advanced setup — 13–18
- The family mystery becomes more coherent.
- The companion connects memories and clues, but the active family search remains locked.
- A credible indication that someone may have survived can be revealed without starting a rescue mission.
- Her capture/rescue, if it occurs, is about the main adventure and the enemies' interest in her abilities—not an early family quest.

### Preparation arc — 19–24
- The companion can recover stronger evidence: records, symbols, survivor testimony, abandoned belongings, or fragmented memories.
- These discoveries build anticipation for stage 25.
- The game may preserve family clues in the world state, but **must not present a direct family-rescue objective before stage 25**.
- The player should understand that her past matters without being able to immediately solve it.

### Stage 25 — The Search Begins
This is the first stage where the hero actively agrees to help the companion search for her family.

The stage can begin with a major discovery that turns scattered clues into a credible trail. The companion identifies a location, route, convoy record, survivor lead, or hidden sanctuary connected to her family.

From this point:
- the family-search objective becomes active;
- family-related map markers can appear;
- the companion can request investigations and choose between leads;
- family locations become full exploration objectives;
- rescue missions can involve prisons, convoys, ruined settlements, hidden sanctuaries, or enemy strongholds.

## After stage 25
The family arc can expand across many stages rather than being solved immediately.

Possible progression:
- 25: first confirmed trail
- 26–28: follow the trail and uncover what happened
- 29+: locate survivors or evidence of multiple survivors
- later stages: rescue operations, difficult choices, family revelations, and the eventual intersection with the hero's lineage mystery

These numbers are guidance, not a hard ending. New stages can be inserted at any point.

## Important separation from the hero's quest
The companion's family is **not** simply another chapter of the hero's family search.

- The hero searches for the truth of the Thunder Giants and his own ancestry.
- The companion searches for what remains of her own family.
- Their investigations can eventually intersect because the same enemy network may have hunted both bloodlines.
- The intersection should be revealed late, after both mysteries have developed independently.

## Capture and rescue pacing
The companion may still be captured before stage 25. Her capture is about the danger of the world and the enemies' interest in her emerging abilities.

After a capture/rescue sequence before stage 25:
- she returns as a permanent companion;
- she can carry new clues or memories;
- the family mystery can deepen;
- the actual family-search objective remains locked.

## Map and progression rules
Before stage 25:
- no active family-rescue marker;
- no direct family route;
- no quest objective telling the player to find her family;
- only optional environmental clues, memories, and fragments.

At stage 25:
- the gate opens;
- accumulated clues can become actionable;
- a first family lead can be generated;
- revisitable objectives can appear;
- later rescues permanently change the world and dialogue.

## Emotional design
The companion should not repeatedly talk about her family in every stage. Her story should breathe.

Use occasional moments:
- a quiet reaction to a familiar symbol;
- a fragmented childhood memory;
- a discovered object she recognizes;
- a short campfire conversation;
- a decision to keep moving because the time is not right yet.

The player should feel that her family story has been present underneath the adventure for a long time, making the stage-25 decision feel earned.

## Extensibility
Stage 25 is the **earliest active search point**, not the final point of the family story. The world remains open-ended and additional stages can be inserted before, during, or after the family arc.
